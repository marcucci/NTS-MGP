## Document Purpose
This document, NTS Methods and Practices Guidelines (NTS MPG), is attached to the ARRL Public Service Communications Manual (PSCM) as Appendix B, and is the working reference manual on Message Formatting, Message Handling, and Traffic Net Procedures in the NTS and NTSD. It shall be the uniform reference guideline document for STMs, Section/Local NMs, and Area Staff members.

<!-- TOC ANCHOR --><a name="document-authority"></a>
## Document Authority
The NTS MPG document is created and maintained for the three NTS Area Chairs by the Standing MPG Committee, and selected additional staff persons, under the General Procedures sections of the NTS Terms of Reference (NTS TOR) attached to the PSCM as Appendix A.

<!-- TOC ANCHOR --><a name="living-document-nature"></a>
## Living Document Nature
This manual will be a working reference document—subject to change as Amateur Radio continuously improves how the tasks are performed. The methods presented are a reasonably accurate snapshot of current practices. They are indeed practices, and not strict rules, but operators will find it beneficial to have a uniform protocol reference.

<!-- TOC ANCHOR --><a name="historical-preservation"></a>
## Historical Preservation
It is hoped that the wisdom of the founders of the ARRL and its NTS with respect to fundamental operating practices, along with new practices, will be preserved intact in one collection of documentation as the NTS goes forward.

## Integration with Other Services
The distinction between NTS operators and those of ARES/RACES/ACS with respect to traffic handling can be made to vanish through cooperative effort and training. The new and popular use of Radio-email provides access for all of these organizations on a common layer while preserving a bridge between ARES® and the NTS/NTSD.

## FOREWORD - The Art of Third Party Communications

Handling communications for a third party has always been a technology and art form capturing the imagination of people throughout the history of civilization. From smoke signals to semaphore, telegraph to email, the art form has adapted to the prevailing technology of the time.

### Historical Context
The ARRL was founded in 1914, and the NTS net system has developed and adapted over the years. Today the ARRL manages and operates the National Traffic System (NTS/NTSD) to assure a continuing and reliable structure to handle formal message traffic in the US.

### Professional Service Standards
The public and our served agencies appreciate our ability to send formal written message traffic efficiently and accurately. When we combine our traffic handling, radio, and net operating skills to provide professional service we earn their trust.

### System Structure
The current NTS and NTSD systems are structured networks. Any station can determine message routing from a Radiogram's address via the structure, either using knowledge of scheduled nets and their connecting liaisons, or understanding automated NTSD MBO routing operations.

<!-- TOC ANCHOR --><a name="primary-objectives"></a>
## PRIMARY OBJECTIVES

### Message Accuracy
The primary objective is to facilitate transmission of a properly formatted written formal message from point A to point B such that it arrives exactly as written on the original copy, group for group, character for character, space for space.

### Mode Compatibility
Radiogram messages filed in the NTS and NTSD must be capable of being transmitted by any mode without message alteration being required.

### Uniform Operations
Voice and CW nets must be run with methods and protocols to operate effectively and uniformly so that message traffic may be exchanged with efficiency. Stations moving about the country to assist during emergencies benefit from finding such uniform practices throughout the system.

### Station Responsibilities
Stations operating in the NTS and NTSD have the responsibility to:
- Promptly relay messages along
- Deliver messages in a timely fashion
- Service undeliverable messages back to the originator
- Honor handling instructions
- Interface professionally with the public and served agencies
